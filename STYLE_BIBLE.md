# Original Crude Story-Cartoon Style Bible

## Purpose

Use this guide to create **new, original YouTube storytelling illustrations** with the same broad visual language as the supplied references, without copying any specific character, scene, pose, composition, or artwork.

The target is a deliberately simple, funny, hand-drawn 2D cartoon that feels made quickly in a basic paint program. The imperfections are part of the design, not defects to be cleaned up.

---

## Master style prompt

> Original crude hand-drawn 2D cartoon illustration for YouTube storytelling, intentionally simple MS-Paint-like construction, wobbly imperfect near-black outlines, solid flat color fills, very limited shading, simple geometric objects, exaggerated stick-figure characters, oversized simple heads, minimal expressive facial features, basic hair shapes, muted restrained palette, uncluttered background, simplified one-point perspective, humorous visual exaggeration, amateur-looking drawing quality, consistent character proportions, consistent line thickness, clear readable silhouette, simple storytelling composition, original character and original scene.

Use this paragraph as the visual foundation, then add the subject, action, setting, mood, and camera direction.

## 1. Drawing language

### Line work

- Use a single dark ink color, almost black rather than pure black: `#171717` or `#1D1D1D`.
- Keep the apparent line weight consistent, approximately 4–7 px in a 1920×1080 image.
- Lines should be visibly hand-drawn: slightly shaky, uneven, and occasionally imperfect at corners.
- Allow small overlaps, blunt endpoints, awkward joins, and mild asymmetry.
- Do not use polished vector curves, calligraphic brush variation, or realistic contour rendering.
- Keep important silhouettes readable even when the line is wobbly.

### Shapes and fills

- Build everything from circles, ovals, rectangles, trapezoids, simple polygons, and loose freehand strokes.
- Fill shapes with one flat color at a time.
- Leave tiny irregular gaps or overlaps where a rushed paint-program drawing might naturally have them.
- Avoid texture, crosshatching, glossy highlights, and smooth digital gradients.
- Add at most one broad shadow shape when it improves readability.

---

## 2. Character construction

- Use exaggerated stick-figure bodies with thin straight or slightly bent limbs.
- Make the head large: approximately 35–45% of the character's total height.
- Use a simple round, oval, or slightly lopsided head with a clear dark outline.
- Keep the torso narrow and uncomplicated; use simple lines or a plain filled shape.
- Draw hands and feet as small rounded marks, simple mitten shapes, or blunt line ends.
- Use short, chunky, geometric hair shapes with only a few uneven points or tufts.
- Keep character proportions consistent across a sequence unless exaggeration is the joke.
- Use body angle, limb direction, and head tilt to communicate emotion more than anatomy.

### Face design

- Use two minimal dark eyes: dots, short ovals, or simple vertical shapes.
- Use one small mouth mark: line, curve, zigzag, oval, or tiny open shape.
- Add only a few expressive marks such as sweat drops, blush marks, raised brows, motion lines, or a single tear.
- Facial expressions should read immediately at thumbnail size.
- Do not add realistic eyes, detailed noses, lips, skin texture, or complex hair rendering.

### Emotional exaggeration

Make the emotion visually obvious and slightly ridiculous:

- panic: tilted head, sweat drops, raised arms, tiny shaking marks
- confusion: uneven eyes, crooked mouth, head tilt, question marks
- exhaustion: drooping posture, half-closed eyes, slumped limbs
- confidence: chest forward, simple grin, wide stance
- embarrassment: hunched shoulders, blush marks, eyes looking away

Use one or two strong exaggerations rather than many small details.

---

## 3. Color system

Use a restrained palette with low-to-medium saturation. Choose one accent color per scene and keep most other colors quiet.

Suggested base palette:

- ink: `#171717`
- warm paper white: `#F1EEE7`
- cool light gray: `#C9C9C6`
- muted medium gray: `#929292`
- dusty blue: `#829DA3`
- muted brown: `#6A332A`
- dark brown: `#3B211D`
- dull ochre: `#A67C3C`
- muted sage: `#87927C`
- restrained red accent: `#9A4D45`

Rules:

- Use 3–7 main colors in a frame.
- Keep backgrounds lighter or quieter than the subject.
- Reserve the strongest accent for the emotional or narrative focal point.
- Use flat fills first; if shading is needed, use one nearby darker flat shape.
- Avoid neon colors, metallic effects, cinematic color grading, and high-contrast photoreal lighting.

---

## 4. Objects and environments

- Construct furniture, rooms, devices, signs, and props from basic geometric forms.
- Simplify objects until their purpose is readable from their silhouette.
- Use slightly imperfect rectangles and trapezoids instead of precise CAD geometry.
- Indicate depth with overlapping flat shapes, a floor line, a wall edge, or one simple vanishing direction.
- Keep perspective basic and occasionally a little awkward on purpose.
- Use only the environmental details needed to support the joke or story beat.
- Do not fill empty space with decorative clutter.

### Background treatment

- Prefer a plain wall, floor, sky, room, or single-color field.
- Use 2–4 large background shapes rather than many small details.
- Keep the horizon or major perspective lines simple and clear.
- Avoid photorealistic depth of field, complex textures, atmospheric haze, and detailed scenery.

---

## 5. Composition for YouTube storytelling

- Default canvas: 16:9 widescreen.
- Keep one obvious focal subject or visual action.
- Use a simple foreground, middle ground, and background when depth is useful.
- Leave clean negative space around faces and important props.
- Make the story legible at thumbnail size.
- Prefer medium-wide and wide shots; use close-ups only for a strong reaction gag.
- Stage the visual joke clearly: setup, reaction, contrast, or absurd consequence.
- Use body direction and object placement to guide the eye.
- Avoid elaborate cinematic framing or crowded ensemble scenes.
- Keep compositions original: vary camera angle, subject placement, environment, action, and prop arrangement from the references.

---

## 6. Humor and storytelling

- Favor visual understatement paired with an exaggerated reaction.
- Use awkward scale, overly large props, tiny furniture, stiff poses, or absurdly simple symbols.
- Make the character's reaction stronger than the event when appropriate.
- Let the crude construction contribute to the punchline.
- Communicate the premise in one readable frame without relying on tiny text.
- If text is necessary, use very short hand-lettered labels with imperfect alignment.

## 9. Consistency checklist

Before accepting an image, confirm:

- [ ] The character and scene are original, not a redraw of a reference.
- [ ] The image reads clearly at thumbnail size.
- [ ] The outlines are wobbly but still readable.
- [ ] The colors are flat, muted, and limited.
- [ ] The background is simple and uncluttered.
- [ ] The face uses only a few strong features.
- [ ] The character proportions match the rest of the sequence.
- [ ] The line thickness feels consistent.
- [ ] The joke or emotional beat is visible immediately.
- [ ] No specific reference pose, composition, prop arrangement, or character design has been reproduced.

---

## 10. Originality guardrail

To keep every new image safely distinct, deliberately change at least four of these items from any reference:

1. character identity and silhouette
2. hairstyle or head shape
3. action and body language
4. setting and major objects
5. camera angle and framing
6. subject placement
7. palette accent
8. emotional beat
9. perspective direction
10. scale relationship between character and props

The goal is to preserve the **visual grammar**—crude lines, flat colors, simple shapes, expressive stick figures, and comedic clarity—not the content of a particular artwork.
