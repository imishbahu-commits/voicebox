# Research — Tomato Shock (lycopene)

Every claim in the script traces to a line here. Sources are human studies
unless marked otherwise.

## The compound

- Lycopene is the carotenoid pigment that makes tomatoes red.
- Lycopene is the most efficient single oxygen quencher among natural
  carotenoids; it neutralises more than 10x more oxygenated free radicals
  than vitamin E. — Cornell University, Liu Rui Hai
  https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2002/04/cooking-tomatoes-boosts-disease-fighting-power

## Mistake 1 — eating it raw (heat frees it)

- In raw tomato, lycopene is locked inside a crystalline matrix within the
  cell wall. The intestine cannot reach it.
- Heat breaks the cell walls and converts some trans-lycopene into
  cis-lycopene, the isomer the body absorbs far more easily.
- Cornell (Liu, Dewanto, Adom, Wu), *Journal of Agriculture and Food
  Chemistry*, 2002. Tomatoes heated to 88 C:
  - trans-lycopene content: +54% at 2 min, +171% at 15 min, +164% at 30 min
  - cis-lycopene (the absorbable form): +6%, +17%, +35%
  - total antioxidant activity: +28%, +34%, +62%
  - vitamin C: -10%, -15%, -29%
  https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2002/04/cooking-tomatoes-boosts-disease-fighting-power

## Mistake 2 — cooking it without fat (fat carries it)

- Lycopene is fat-soluble. Without lipid in the same meal it passes through
  unabsorbed even once heat has freed it.
- Fielding et al. 2005, RCT, n=23: 470 g tomatoes cooked WITH 25 ml olive
  oil raised plasma lycopene by 82%. The group cooked WITHOUT oil showed
  **no significant increase** in trans-lycopene (P=0.684).
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2161831322008675
- Gartner et al. 1997, *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, crossover
  trial: the same lycopene dose from tomato paste vs fresh tomatoes, both
  with fat. Paste gave 2.5x the peak blood concentration and 3.8x the total
  amount absorbed, measured in the chylomicron fraction.
- The crossover trial found no significant difference for alpha-carotene or
  beta-carotene — the effect is specific to lycopene's crystalline structure.

## Second mechanism — skin (the surprise)

- Lycopene accumulates in the skin.
- Stahl et al. 2001, *Journal of Nutrition* 131:1449-1451: 40 g tomato paste
  (~16 mg lycopene) with 10 g olive oil daily for 10 weeks, 9 volunteers;
  controls (n=10) had olive oil only. UV-induced erythema was **40% lower**
  in the tomato paste group (P=0.02). No significant difference at week 4 —
  the effect needs ~10 weeks of steady intake.
  https://jn.nutrition.org/article/S0022-3166(22)13819-8/pdf
- Rizwan et al. 2010, *British Journal of Dermatology*, RCT: 20 healthy
  women, 55 g tomato paste (16 mg lycopene) in olive oil vs olive oil alone
  daily for 12 weeks — protection against erythema, extracellular matrix
  changes and mitochondrial DNA damage.
  https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1365-2133.2010.10057.x
- **Honesty beat:** the protection is roughly equivalent to SPF 1.3-1.5.
  Dermatologists recommend SPF 30+. This does NOT replace sunscreen. No
  dietary carotenoid has been shown to prevent skin cancer.

## Third mechanism — blood pressure

- Meta-analysis of 6 intervention trials (*Nutrients* 2013, 5(9):3696):
  lycopene supplementation lowered systolic blood pressure by a mean of
  **4.95 mmHg** (95% CI -8.82 to -1.09, p=0.012). No statistically
  significant effect on diastolic (-3.81, p=0.087).
  https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/5/9/3696
- Doses above 12 mg/day were more effective, and the effect appeared in
  people with elevated baseline BP — not in people whose BP was already
  normal.
- Proposed mechanisms: ACE inhibition reducing angiotensin-II oxidative
  stress and indirectly enhancing nitric oxide production; adiponectin
  modulation.

## Cardiovascular / LDL

- Review (whole food vs supplement, *Journal of Agricultural and Food
  Chemistry* / ScienceDirect): improvement in resistance of LDL to oxidation
  after tomato products delivering as little as ~8 mg lycopene/day for
  3 weeks.
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2161831322008675

## What NOT to claim

- Do not call it a sunscreen replacement (effect is ~SPF 1.3).
- Do not claim it prevents skin cancer or prostate cancer — human evidence
  does not support that.
- Do not claim a large blood-pressure drop in people with normal BP; the
  effect was seen in prehypertensive/hypertensive groups.
- The 10-week skin effect is slow and steady-state, not same-day.
